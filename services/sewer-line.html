Sewer line

For backups that feel bigger than one drain.

When more than one fixture is affected, customers usually need a more careful provider conversation. This guide explains how to describe sewer-line symptoms without getting buried in contractor language.

Start a requestAll services
What people notice

Toilets bubble, a basement drain overflows, several fixtures slow down, or a strong odor appears near drains.

What helps

Whether the backup is active, where the cleanout is, if trees are nearby, and whether this has happened before.

What to compare

Whether the provider discusses clearing only, camera inspection, repair options, cleanup boundaries, and written exclusions.

Why this request deserves more context

A sewer concern can quickly move from annoying to disruptive because it may affect bathrooms, laundry, and the ability to use water normally. Customers do not need to know the exact cause before asking for help. They do need to describe the pattern: which fixtures are involved, whether sewage is present, whether the issue returns, and whether there is an accessible cleanout.

Independent providers may talk about clearing the line, checking it with a camera, locating the problem, or discussing repair paths. Those are different scopes, so customers should compare what each provider is actually proposing.

Information to gather before authorizing repair

For larger sewer work, customers can ask for a camera recording, marked location, depth estimate, repair method, access plan, permit requirements, restoration responsibilities, warranty terms, and whether the quote covers only pipe work or also landscaping, flooring, concrete, or cleanup. Comparing scopes is important because sewer estimates can look similar while excluding different items.

  • Ask whether camera inspection is included or separate.
  • Confirm whether the issue is on private property or near a municipal connection.
  • Clarify excavation, trenchless, or spot repair assumptions.
  • Request written exclusions for restoration and cleanup.
  • Confirm timing if wastewater use must be limited.
